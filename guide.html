What this tool is

Ledger Line Reports is a companion tool for your QuickFile account. You give it a backup zip file exported from QuickFile, and it works out reports that QuickFile's own screens don't currently show — line-by-line invoice detail, monthly summaries by property or nominal code, profit by rental property, year-on-year comparisons, and bank reconciliation.

It runs entirely in your web browser: there's nothing to install, no account or sign-up, and it works alongside your normal QuickFile account rather than replacing it — QuickFile is still where you raise invoices, record payments and file your VAT return. This tool is an independent, complementary view of the same data, for questions QuickFile's built-in reports don't answer directly. It isn't an official QuickFile feature.

Where your numbers come from

Everything you see comes from the single backup zip file you choose — the same backup you can export at any time from within QuickFile. That backup contains a full copy of your invoices, purchases, nominal ledger and bank statement lines, all as a set of files QuickFile writes out for you.

When you load the zip, this tool reads those files and matches them up the way an accountant would: it takes each invoice and pulls in the customer or supplier it belongs to, the account (nominal code) each line was posted to, and the property or job it was tagged against — then adds all of that detail alongside the line-by-line figures. For the bank reports, it reads your bank statement lines directly, and separately checks your nominal ledger just to work out which bank account is which (its name and accounting code) — never to work out the balances themselves, which always come from the statement lines alone.

Nothing is uploaded anywhere. The zip file is opened and read entirely on your own computer, inside this browser tab — it's never sent to QuickFile, to Anthropic, or to any other server. If you close the tab or refresh the page, the data is gone and you'd need to choose the zip again; nothing is saved between visits.

Using the tool, step by step

  1. Export a backup from QuickFile. In QuickFile, go to Account Settings → All Settings → Monthly Backups, enter an email address, and click Run Now. You'll receive an email shortly afterwards with a link to download your backup — save the zip file somewhere you can find it (you don't need to unzip it).
    The Monthly backups option under Account Settings → All Settings.
    The Backup Settings page — enter your email under "Send to" and click "Run now".
  2. Choose the zip file. Click the "Choose backup .zip" button near the top of the page and select the file you just downloaded. Once it's loaded, the page shows your own data instead of the example figures.
  3. Pick a report. Use the Report dropdown to choose what you want to see — line-item detail, a monthly summary, profit by project, a year-on-year comparison, or one of the bank reports. A short description under the dropdown explains what that report shows.
  4. Narrow it down. Most reports have a search box and a few filters (client or supplier, nominal code, status, a date range, or a "12 months ending"/checkpoint date picker, depending on the report). These update the table instantly.
  5. Sort and review. Click any column heading to sort by that column; click it again to reverse the order.
  6. Export what you're looking at. Click Copy CSV to copy exactly what's on screen — filtered and sorted — to your clipboard, then paste it straight into Excel or Google Sheets. There's no downloadable file; copy-and-paste is how results leave the tool.

If you'd like to see how it behaves before loading a real backup, the tool opens with example figures already in place — everything above works the same way against that example data.

Reporting on project tags? Sales by project, Profit by project, and anything else grouped by property or job need project data to be in the backup. When you create the backup, tick the Include Projects box — it isn't ticked by default, and without it your project tags won't come through.

Line-item detail reports

These two reports show one row per invoice line — the most granular view available — rather than a total per invoice.

Sales invoice items

Lists every line from every sales invoice, together with the client it was billed to, the property or job it was tagged against, and the invoice's status (for example paid in full, sent, or refunded). Use the Search box to find a line by item name, description, nominal name or tag; use Client, Nominal code, Status and the From/To date fields to narrow the list down.

Purchase invoice items

Shows the same kind of detail for the purchase (expense) side, with the supplier in place of the client. QuickFile's own purchase export doesn't include a quantity or unit cost for each line, so those columns aren't shown here either — only the description and the amount.

Monthly summary reports

These reports group your sales or purchase lines into one row per project, item, account, client or supplier, with a column for each of the 12 months ending a month you choose, plus a Total column and a Total row at the bottom.

  • Sales by project — total sales income for each tagged property or job, month by month.
  • Quantity sold by item — how many of each item or line description were sold each month.
  • Sales by item — total sales income for each item or line description, month by month.
  • Sales by nominal — total sales income posted to each nominal (account) code.
  • Sales by client — total sales income from each client.
  • Purchases by supplier — total spend with each supplier.
  • Purchases by nominal — total spend posted to each nominal (account) code.

Use the "12 months ending" picker to choose which month the 12-month window finishes on — the tool always shows the full 12 months leading up to it. Every property, item, account, client or supplier that appears anywhere in your data is listed, even if it had no activity in the months currently on screen — it simply shows as zero rather than disappearing. The Search box filters the list by name.

Profit by project

This report is aimed squarely at property or job profitability: for each property or job tag, it shows net rental (or sales) income minus net costs — repairs, utilities and anything else recorded against that same tag — side by side, month by month, for the 12 months ending a month you choose.

Each month's profit is that month's sales income for the tag minus that month's purchase costs for the same tag, so a property showing a negative figure in a given month simply cost more than it earned that month — check the Total column for the full 12-month picture. Every property or job tag seen anywhere in your sales or purchase data gets a row, even if it only ever appears on one side (all income, or all cost).

Year-on-year comparisons

These four reports place five years side by side — plus a Total column and a Total row — so you can compare how income or spending has moved over time, rather than looking at one 12-month window at a time.

  • Income by year (calendar) and Expenditure by year (calendar) compare five calendar years (January to December), ending the year you choose.
  • Income by year (custom year-end) and Expenditure by year (custom year-end) instead compare five rolling 12-month periods, each ending on whatever date you pick — useful if your business's year doesn't run January to December.

Each report can be grouped a different way using the Group by dropdown: income can be grouped by project, nominal code, client or item; expenditure by project, nominal code, supplier or category. Use the Search box to filter the rows by name, and click a column heading to sort by that year's figures.

Bank reports

These two reports are for checking your recorded bank balances against your real-life bank statements, using the bank statement lines from your backup — never figures from your invoices.

Bank account balances

Shows one row per bank account, with its running balance at a series of checkpoints leading up to a date you choose. You can switch how far apart those checkpoints are: Monthly (the last 12 months), Half-monthly (the 15th and last day of each month, covering about 6 months), or Yearly (the last 12 years). This makes it easy to check whether your balance at a specific point in time — including partway through a month — matches what your bank statement says, and to narrow down when the two started to disagree if they don't.

Daily bank balances

Shows the same running balance for every single day within one calendar month — pick any date and the tool shows the whole month it falls in. Use this once the checkpoint report has pointed you toward a particular month, to pin a discrepancy down to an exact day.

Each account is labelled with its nominal (accounting) code and name where the tool can identify it confidently from your backup; if it can't be matched with confidence, the account is still shown — just without a code or name — so you never lose an account from the report, only its label.
